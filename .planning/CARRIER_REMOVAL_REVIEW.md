# Carrier removal in 4H-SiC: literature review and model check

**Date:** 2026-10-21. **Base:** fd89b2e. **Scope:** why notebook 14 shows almost no CCE
loss below the compensation fluence (CCE 0.998 at 0.9 Phi_comp, -40 V, R^2 -0.38 against
its reference curve), and whether the model's carrier removal is wrong or only
mis-parameterised.

**Verdict.** The removal model is not physically wrong for this question, and it needs no
re-parameterisation to explain nb14. The nb14 "onset" expectation is the thing that is
wrong. Carrier removal cannot reduce CCE at -40 V below Phi_comp, because the 10 um epi stays
over-depleted (V_fd about 3 V, falling as N_D is removed). The CCE loss there is trapping
only, and trapping only becomes significant well above 1e13 p/cm^2. The reference curve
in nb14 cell 8 (CCE 0.95 / 0.70 / 0.40 at 1e12 / 1e13 / 5e13 p/cm^2) is not a measurement.
It is attributed to Burin et al., a neutron TCAD paper with no 62 MeV CCE data. Measured
SiC CCE loss starts at about 1e13-1e15 neq. The real model gap is beyond Phi_comp, not
below it: the model refuses those points, while real SiC goes semi-insulating and keeps
detecting. **No code change is made** (rationale in section 5).

## 1. How the code models it (fd89b2e)

- `etna/core/radiation_damage.py`:
  - `RadiationDamageParams.eta_removal = 5.0` cm^-1 per Si 1-MeV-neq.
  - `apply_carrier_removal` does N_D(x) -> max(N_D(x) - eta * kappa(E) * Phi, 0). It is
    linear, doping-independent and position-wise.
  - The model adds no acceptors and does not pin the Fermi level with Z1/2 or EH6/7.
    Defects act only through SRH lifetimes, via `compute_K_tau`:
    K_n = 1.76e-5 cm^2/s and K_p = 4.16e-6 cm^2/s.
- Provenance of 5.0: 13-RESEARCH.md says it was set equal to eta_Z12 ("the Burin
  calibration point"). PHYSICS_AUDIT_v4 M9 flagged this as unjustified. Burin et al.
  (arXiv:2407.16710) is a TCAD paper on neutron-irradiated diodes. I checked the full
  text (HTML v1): it has no proton 4H-SiC data, no removal rate, and no CCE results ("we
  will not explicitly show the achieved CCE results here"). Its Table I introduction
  rates are taken from Hazdra et al. (2014).
- `compute_phi_crit` and `etna.api.simulation.compensation_fluence` give
  Phi_comp = min N_D / (eta * kappa). For the default stack (N_D_bulk 3.38e13,
  kappa(62 MeV) = 1.942) that is **3.48e12 p/cm^2**. Points at or beyond Phi_comp are
  returned as NaN with a reason (`full_compensation_reason`).
- The CCE sweep (`cce_vs_fluence`) has alpha generation confined to the epi and gets
  damage from lifetimes plus N_D only.

## 2. Discriminating calculation (scratch, not in repo)

The script `trap_vs_removal.py` was session scratch and is not kept. The formula and the
parameters here are enough to reproduce it. It uses the first-order Hecht loss
t_transit / (2 tau) per carrier, with uniform generation, E = 40 kV/cm and saturated
drift. Transit times are 78 ps for electrons and 285 ps for holes.

| N_D_bulk          | Phi_comp (62 MeV) | at 0.9 Phi_comp: tau_n / tau_p | trapping CCE loss | bulk V_fd left |
| ----------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------- | -------------- |
| 3.38e13 (default) | 3.48e12           | 9.3 / 39.5 ns                  | about 0.4 %       | 0.3 V          |
| 5e13              | 5.15e12           | 6.3 / 26.7 ns                  | about 0.6 %       | 0.5 V          |
| 1e14 (documented) | 1.03e13           | 3.2 / 13.4 ns                  | about 1.1 %       | 0.9 V          |

- Trapping alone gives 1 % loss at about 8e12 p/cm^2 and 20 % at about 1.6e14 p/cm^2
  (62 MeV). These fluences do not depend on N_D.
- The simulator's 0.2 % at 0.9 Phi_comp agrees with this first-order estimate within a
  factor of 2 (the field is non-uniform and the saturation velocities are assumed).
- So raising N_D_bulk to 1e14 moves the refusal point out by 3x. It would make about 1 %
  loss visible just below the new Phi_comp. It does **not** produce a 1e11-1e12 onset.
- No plausible removal rate does that either. A higher eta only moves the refusal point
  earlier, and the device stays over-depleted until that point.

**Doping dependence.** The only same-beam, same-method comparison found is Radmanovac et
al. It gives 4.5 +- 0.3 at 4e14 and 5.6 +- 0.8 at 4e13 (per proton). That is a factor
10 in N_D with no significant change, and if anything slightly higher at lower doping.

- A rate proportional to N_D (the "removal ∝ n0 × NIEL" statement that circulates for SiC)
  is **not** supported in the 4e13-4e14 window that matters here.
- No paper that I could open gives fitted parameters for a doping-dependent rate.

**Particle and energy dependence.** At 252.7 MeV the model's eta = 5 per Si-neq matches
the data within 0-50 %, which is partly luck given how 5.0 was chosen.

- 15 MeV protons (Lebedev 2017) and reactor neutrons (Hazdra 2016) give 2.6-3.7x more
  removal per Si-neq. Both used commercial Schottky/JBS power-diode drift layers, probably
  doped at 1e15 or more (not stated in the text I could access). The excess is therefore
  EITHER a particle/NIEL dependence OR a doping dependence above about 1e15, and these
  data cannot tell which. It is not evidence that the 62 MeV rate on a 3e13-1e14 epi is
  3x higher.
- I found no measured 62 MeV removal rate. The model's Si-NIEL interpolation gives
  9.7 /p. If the Lebedev/Hazdra excess turned out to be a particle effect, the true value
  could be up to roughly 2x higher, giving Phi_comp(default) between 3.5e12 and about
  1.7e12 p/cm^2. If it is a doping effect, the model value stands.

**CCE versus fluence: the measured onset.**

- Gsponer et al., arXiv:2310.02047: 50 um, 1.5e14, TRIGA. Alpha CCE stays above 80 % up to
  1e15 neq in reverse bias. There is no removal rate because C-V is flat after
  irradiation. The detectors still work in forward and reverse bias, beyond full
  compensation.
- De Napoli 2022, a review of several sources: neutron CCE "remaining very high only
  until some 1e14 n/cm^2". Losses of 7 % and 22.5 % are reported at 1.3e14 and 7.3e14
  (14 MeV n, 20 um, 1e14 doping). Electron and proton CCE degrade significantly only
  around 1e14-1e15 cm^-2.
- The model's trapping-only estimate is 20 % loss at 1.6e14 p/cm^2 (3e14 neq). That is in
  the same decade as these measurements. Nothing measured supports a 1 % onset at
  1e11-1e12 p/cm^2.

## 4. Diagnosis

1. **The nb14 expectation is wrong (the main finding).**
   - Cell 2 lists "Burin_2024: 62 MeV, 10 um, this study", with onset "~1e12" and
     significant loss "~1e13". Burin et al. is a neutron TCAD paper (full text checked):
     it has no proton data and no CCE numbers.
   - Cell 8's reference curve (0.95 / 0.70 / 0.40) is labelled "approximate" and has no
     source data.
   - The R^2 of -0.38 therefore compares the model with an invented curve. It is the same
     pattern as the fabricated kappa in nb18/19 (PHYSICS_REVIEW_v6).
   - The Moscatelli 2017 / "Raffi 2021, Sensors 21, 1048" references in cell 2 were not
     verified in this review.
2. **Below Phi_comp the model behaves correctly.** Removal lowers V_fd, the detector stays
   over-depleted at -40 V, and the loss is trapping-limited (a few tenths of a percent).
3. **eta_removal = 5 / neq is mis-sourced but not mis-valued at high energy.**
   - The Radmanovac data happen to back it at 252.7 MeV.
   - At 62 MeV the true value could be up to about 2-3x higher, but only if the higher
     low-energy-proton and neutron rates are a particle effect and not a
     high-doping effect (undetermined). That would move Phi_comp earlier, to as low as
     about 1.2-1.7e12 p/cm^2. It would not create an onset.
4. **The actual deficiency is beyond Phi_comp.**
   - The model has no compensated state: N_D floors at 0 and the point is refused.
   - Measured 4H-SiC becomes semi-insulating, with E_F pinned by Z1/2 / EH6/7 and no type
     inversion. It still collects charge to 1e15-1e16 neq, with trapping-dominated CCE.
   - Correct physics here needs deep acceptor traps as charged species in the Poisson
     equation (Z1/2 acceptor at Ec-0.67, EH6/7 at Ec-1.6, densities eta_i * Phi), instead of
     a donor subtraction. This is the Burin et al. TCAD approach. It is a model change,
     not a parameter fix.
5. **N_D_bulk 3.4e13 vs 0.5-1e14** shifts Phi_comp by up to 3x (3.48e12 -> 1.03e13), but
   by section 2 it cannot rescue the nb14 onset either way.

## 5. Recommendation (nothing implemented)

- **No doping-dependent removal rate.** The one directly comparable dataset shows none
  over 4e13-4e14, and no accessible paper gives fitted parameters. An opt-in flag would
  also not change nb14 at -40 V.
- **Fix nb14's expectation (presentation, cheap).**
  - Remove or relabel the invented reference curve and the "Burin 2024: 62 MeV, this
    study" entry.
  - Replace the onset band with the measured decade, about 1e13-1e15 neq for 4H-SiC
    detectors (cite Gsponer 2310.02047 and De Napoli 2022), converted with kappa.
  - State that below Phi_comp an over-depleted 10 um device is trapping-limited by
    construction.
- **Re-cite eta_removal.** Cite Radmanovac et al. (arXiv:2510.11304; 252.7 MeV p,
  4.5-5.6 /p, about 5-6.6 /Si-neq) instead of "= eta_Z12". Note that 15 MeV p and reactor
  neutrons on power-diode drift layers give about 3x more per Si-neq (Lebedev 2017,
  Hazdra 2016). Whether that comes from the particle or from the doping is undetermined,
  so the 62 MeV value carries an upper uncertainty of up to about x2-3.
- **Future model work.** Represent compensation by deep acceptor traps in Poisson (Z1/2,
  EH6/7, the Burin-style TCAD approach), so that fluences beyond Phi_comp are solvable.
  Validate against the semi-insulating behaviour: flat C-V, CCE still above 50 % at
  1e15 neq.

## 6. INFN-LNS data that would settle it

On the actual Petringa / LNS devices (10 um epi, 4 mm^2), irradiated at 62 MeV (CATANA)
at 4-5 fluences spanning about 0.1-3x the predicted Phi_comp (about 3e11 - 1e13 p/cm^2):

1. **C-V before and after each step.** 1/C^2 slopes at matched depletion depth (the
   Radmanovac method) give a direct 62 MeV removal rate at this doping. With
   pre-irradiation C-V and SIMS or SRP of the same wafer, this also resolves N_D_bulk
   (3.4e13 from the fit vs the documented 0.5-1e14).
2. **CCE vs bias** (alpha and protons) at the same fluences, including beyond the C-V
   flattening. This tests the trapping constants (K_tau) and the post-compensation regime
   the model currently refuses.
3. **DLTS** of Z1/2 and EH6/7 versus fluence: introduction rates at 62 MeV, which sets the
   ratio of removal to Z1/2.
4. **Leakage I-V at 300 K** versus fluence: an independent check of the generation term.
